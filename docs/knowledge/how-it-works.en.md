# How Itqan works

## The steps

1. **You upload your CV.** A transcript is optional and worth adding: coursework is
   evidence of skills your CV may never mention.
2. **Itqan reads your documents** and shows you what it found — your details, your
   qualifications, the skills it could evidence.
3. **You check it and correct anything wrong.** Nothing is matched until you have. This
   step is deliberate: Itqan works from what you confirmed, not from what a machine
   guessed about you.
4. **Itqan matches you** against job postings currently open, and works out which skills
   those roles asked for that your documents do not evidence.
5. **Itqan recommends courses** — one per missing skill, where a course for it exists.

## Where the jobs come from

Public job postings from Omani sources, refreshed every 12 hours. Postings are checked
for the signs of a scam before they reach you, and duplicates of the same vacancy are
collapsed into one.

The course catalogue is refreshed every three days.

## How skills are compared

The hard part is that a job posting and a course describe the same skill in different
words. Itqan maps both onto ESCO, the European Union's standard skills vocabulary, so
that what employers ask for and what courses teach are counted in the same units and can
actually be compared.

When two skills are related but not identical — a job asking for "machine learning"
against a CV listing TensorFlow and scikit-learn — Itqan resolves that too, and records
which of your skills satisfied the requirement so you can see the reasoning.

## Reading your documents

Itqan reads PDFs and photographs of documents, including scanned pages. Every detail it
extracts is checked back against the text of your document: if it cannot point at where
in your CV something came from, it does not claim it.
